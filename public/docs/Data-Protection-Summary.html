01

Scope & roles

This summary covers personal data collected through nextVillage’s learning platform — accounts, learning activity, AI chat/tutoring content, assessment results, safety records, and community-impact data — for learners in partner communities such as Maji Mazuri. It maps that data against Kenya’s Data Protection Act, 2019 (“the Act”) and its General Regulations, 2021. The platform also serves learners in Nigeria and the United States, so the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 and the US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) apply as well; the platform is run to the strictest of them.

02

What we collect

Account & identity
Name, email, school, town or city, country, join code / organization affiliation, role (learner, teacher, leader, admin), and the learner’s birth year (year only, never a full birthday) — used to apply the right protections.
Guardian consent record
For learners under 18: who confirmed a parent or guardian’s consent, when, how (signed form, in person, other), and a short note. No guardian names or contact details are kept on the platform; the signed forms stay with the program.
Learning activity
Module progress, certifications earned, community-impact challenge submissions.
AI conversation content
Prompts and messages a learner sends to the platform’s AI tutor/chat features, and the model’s responses — scrubbed of third-party PII before it reaches a model; see §08. Cleared on a schedule — see §04.
Messages & media
Messages and shared images in Together rooms (off for children under 13 unless their group opts in); images, audio, and video a learner creates.
Assessment & research data
Proficiency assessment results (baseline and monthly); longitudinal research data explored by youth researchers under k-anonymization (minimum group size 5).
Community records about other people
Where an older learner or adult helper supports a patient, farmer, fisher, or business owner, the platform stores that person’s name, village, and consultation notes. Children under 13 cannot create these records. Health records have identifiers removed when a consultation is finished.
Safety records & privacy requests
When a message is flagged, the category and an excerpt are logged and emailed to a leader — see §09. Learners’ deletion, withdrawal, and copy requests are recorded — see §05.

05

Deletion requests

Live Replaces the “manual only” finding of earlier revisions.

How to ask
On the profile page (“Privacy and my data”): delete my account and data, withdraw permission (for a parent or guardian), or send me a copy. Or by email to the contact on the privacy notice.
Who can ask
The learner, or a parent or guardian for a minor.
What happens
The request appears for the learner’s leaders and for administrators. A leader can lock the account immediately. An administrator then erases the account and every stored file (npm run user:delete, dry run first), and database records cascade.
Turnaround
A reply within 14 days (7 days if the request is only for a copy of the learner’s data), as Kenya’s General Regulations require; the account and files are fully erased within 30 days. Both are stated on the privacy notice.
What cannot be removed
The person’s name inside another person’s own notes or messages; safety records already made; anonymized research data already folded into a group of five or more; and copies held by a vendor under its own rules.
06

Cross-border transfer

In progress A notice is in place; the paperwork behind it is not finished.

Every message a learner sends leaves their country — to Supabase and Anthropic in the US, through Vercel’s US functions, and for some pages on to a second provider. That’s ordinary for a SaaS product; the laws require a lawful basis for it.

Sections 48–49 of the Kenyan Act permit a cross-border transfer on one of four bases: an adequacy finding for the destination country, appropriate safeguards (such as standard contractual clauses in the vendor’s data processing agreement), necessity for performing a contract with the data subject, or the data subject’s consent combined with confirmation that safeguards are in place. Nigeria’s Act has comparable mechanisms (adequacy, contractual clauses, consent, and others).

Done: a public privacy notice (nextvillage.community/privacy) says plainly that learners’ information is processed outside their country and by whom. To do: collect and file the data processing agreements of each vendor (§13, item 03) and have counsel confirm the legal basis for each country.

On moving hosting to Africa

As of 12 September 2026, Supabase did not offer an African hosting region; AWS’s Cape Town region was briefly available in an early Supabase alpha and discontinued. Re-check this before relying on it. If it still holds, the pragmatic path is not to chase regional hosting — it’s to make the existing US hosting lawful: sign data processing agreements with each vendor and keep the plain-language notice current. Reserve true data residency (self-hosting Postgres in an African region) for if a funder or regulator makes it a hard requirement; it’s a materially larger project.

07

AI providers & learner inputs

Tighter — terms still being confirmed A written vendor review was completed on 15 October 2026.

Claude (Anthropic) — the default path, and the only AI for children under 13

The platform’s learner-facing chat, tutoring, assessment, scrubbing, and safety classification call Claude models (Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5) through the standard Claude API under Anthropic’s commercial terms. Under those terms, API content is not used to train Anthropic’s models. Prompts and outputs are automatically deleted after a short period — Anthropic’s documentation has described 30 days and some recent summaries report 7, so the exact figure should be confirmed in the data processing agreement. Content flagged by Anthropic’s safety systems or under a legal hold may be kept longer. Zero Data Retention is available to eligible customers on request. Anthropic also publishes guidance for organizations whose products are used by children; the platform follows its main points (age checks through leader approval, content moderation, monitoring and reporting) and will confirm whether Anthropic’s child-safety system prompt applies.

The free-tier fallback chain, as it is today

Requests from AILearningPage, EnglishSkillsPage, MathSkillsPage, ScienceSkillsPage, SkillsDevelopmentPage, AgricultureConsultantPage, FishingConsultantPage, EntrepreneurshipConsultantPage, AIAmbassadorsPage, and PidginTranslationModule route through a cost-driven chain — Groq → Cerebras → Cloudflare Workers AI → Mistral → Anthropic Haiku as the final, paid fallback. OpenRouter, DeepSeek, and Gemini have been removed. Coding pages, certification/evaluation pages, and the Healthcare Navigator route to Anthropic directly, with no fallback chain.

Children under 13

A learner who counts as under 13 is never routed through the free-tier chain. Their typed text goes to Anthropic only; image, voice, and video generation, speech-to-text, and the other AI providers are switched off for them, and the server refuses those requests even if called directly.

What the vendor review found (15 October 2026)

  • Groq, Cerebras, Cloudflare state they do not train on customer content and keep little or nothing; Groq offers a zero-retention setting.
  • Mistral: sources disagree on whether API data is used for training; the setting is to be checked in Mistral’s admin console, or the provider removed.
  • OpenAI: API data is not used for training unless the customer opts in; abuse-monitoring logs are kept up to 30 days. Not used for under-13s.
  • Replicate deletes API prediction data after one hour by default. ElevenLabs says its service is not directed to children under 13 and may use voice data to improve its systems; to be confirmed for the API.
  • Signed data processing agreements are not yet on file for any vendor (§13, item 03). Vercel, Resend, StackBlitz, and SpeechGen terms are still to be reviewed.

The full review, with sources, is maintained alongside this document.

08

PII scrubbing

Live Applies to /api/chat, /api/chat-stream (the AI Playground’s streaming path), and the AI Image Creation function.

What’s allowed through, and what isn’t

The learner’s own first name is the only personal detail passed through unchanged. Everything else is either removed outright or converted to a relationship term before the message reaches any model:

Other people’s names
Converted to the relationship the text states or implies — “my brother,” “my neighbour,” “my patient” — never left in as a name, and never invented where the text gives none (“someone I know”).
Phone numbers, emails, handles
Removed by a deterministic pattern match, in any common format (Kenyan and Nigerian formats are explicitly covered).
Addresses & locations
Governed by a scrub “tier” — see below.
School names, exact ages/birthdates
Removed in every case.

Two layers

A pattern match (regex) catches phone numbers, emails, and handles first — free, deterministic, and it runs regardless of whether the AI call that follows succeeds. A second pass sends the (already pattern-scrubbed) text to a Claude Haiku call that extracts a short list of “replace this exact phrase with that one” instructions, which the platform applies itself — the model never rewrites a message in full.

Location tiers

TierApplies toLocation handling
strict (default)AI Playground, Create Game, AI Image Creation, everywhere elseNothing more specific than a broad place name (a city, county, region) — a school or exact address is removed
community_helperAgriculture, Fishing, Healthcare Navigator, Entrepreneurship Consultant, AI Ambassadors — and their certification variantsNeighborhood/estate-level detail is kept; a full street address, school name, or P.O. Box is still removed
The precise guarantee — read this carefully

The extraction pass necessarily sends the (already pattern-scrubbed) text to Anthropic, to find what still needs removing — recognizing a name, or inferring “my brother” from context, is a language task a pattern match can’t do. So the accurate claim is “everyone except Anthropic sees only scrubbed text” — not “personal information never leaves the platform.” Anthropic’s commercial terms (§07) apply to that call like any other.

Known gaps

  • An incident, now fixed. Between 9 and 14 October 2026 the model-based second pass (and the safety classifier) failed on a configuration error — newer Claude models reject a setting the code was still sending. The pattern-based layer kept running throughout. The fault was found in a code review, corrected, and re-verified on 14 October.
  • Coverage. Scrubbing covers the three functions named above. A handful of other Supabase Edge Functions that call Anthropic directly for scoring (challenge evaluation, weekly-challenge generation) have not been given this layer; see §13, item 05.
  • The image-creation scrubber is a separate, mirrored copy of the main one, so the two can drift apart as either is updated.
  • Regex address matching is a floor, not a solution: an address with neither a P.O. Box/Plot/House-No. pattern nor a US-style street suffix depends on the second pass.
09

Safety guardrails & escalation

Live Extended since the last revision to cover Together rooms.

How it works

Independently of the AI’s own reply, every AI message is classified by a separate Claude Haiku call into one of four categories: none, self_harm, harm_to_others, or hate_or_discriminatory. The classifier reads the learner’s original, unscrubbed message — deliberately, because an alert reading “I’m going to hurt [name removed]” would be close to useless to the person meant to act on it.

  • In chat: a flag doesn’t block the AI’s own caring reply; it triggers a background alert.
  • In AI Image Creation: a flagged prompt is refused — no image is generated.
  • In Together rooms: messages are checked for the same categories by the database; a Report button lets any learner flag a message; messages are never blocked. Flags appear on the leader’s dashboard, and an email goes to the leaders, retried every ten minutes until sent.

Who gets told, and what they see

A flag emails the learner’s community leader(s) — resolved from the learner’s organization record — naming the category, the page, and a quoted excerpt of what was written. It is labeled as unreviewed and automated — “please follow up with the student” — not a finding of fact. Flags are visible only to leaders and administrators of the learner’s organization.

What this means for partner communities

If a young person says something alarming to the AI or in a group chat, a designated adult at your organization is emailed, automatically, usually within seconds. It is worth confirming who that designated adult is for each partner before launch.

Known gaps

  • The backup alert address is still not set. SAFETY_ALERT_FALLBACK_EMAIL is not configured, so a flag for a learner whose organization has no leader on file reaches no one. This matters more now that some learners joined without a group; administrators have a tool to place them in one. §13, item 02.
  • Alert email from the image-creation function depends on a Resend key in the Supabase project’s secrets; to be verified.
  • Not yet exercised end to end against a real flagged message in a live room; a deliberate test pass is planned.

Children’s data & consent

Built Replaces the “not yet demonstrated” finding of earlier revisions. Section 33 of the Kenyan Act treats anyone under 18 as a child; Nigeria’s Act (s. 31) and COPPA (under 13) are comparable or stricter.

Consent

  • For every learner under 18, a parent or guardian’s consent is collected by the learner’s group leader as a signed form (or recorded in-person agreement) and recorded on the platform: who confirmed it, when, how, and a short note.
  • A new learner cannot be approved by a leader until that record is made. A leader holding forms for a whole group can confirm them together.
  • For learners who joined earlier, leaders record the forms the program already holds, in batches. Nobody loses access for lacking a record while this is completed.
  • The platform keeps no guardian names or contact details; the signed forms remain with the program and should be stored safely.
  • Whether leader attestation fully meets the Kenyan and Nigerian requirement to verify consent is a question for local counsel.

Extra protections for children under 13

  • Other learners see them as “Learner 1234” — no name, no photo — on leaderboards, cohort lists, and in public champion and winner stories.
  • No peer chat, unless the program has chosen to allow it for a supervised group whose parents have been told.
  • One AI provider only (Anthropic); image, voice, and video tools and the free-tier chain are off.
  • They cannot create records about other people.
  • Shorter retention: AI transcripts 60 days; generated media 6 months (§04).

“Under 13” is decided from the birth year the learner or their leader enters (year only, so the younger reading applies at the boundary). A learner with no birth year yet, in a group that includes children under 13, is treated as under 13 until it is known. Leaders can enter a missing birth year themselves.

§08 and §09 are genuine protections worth naming to a guardian, but neither is itself a consent mechanism.

11

Security controls already in place

Solid foundation Strengthened by a security review in October 2026.

  • Encryption in transit and at rest — standard on Supabase-managed Postgres and Vercel-hosted traffic (TLS).
  • Row-Level Security on database tables — access is scoped per user and organization at the database layer, not left to application code alone. Sensitive functions were locked down so signed-out visitors cannot call them.
  • Leader approval of every new member, and a guard that stops anyone changing their own role or organization.
  • Sign-in required on AI routes, with rate limits on code execution; code runs in an isolated sandbox.
  • Leaderboards and rosters limited to the learner’s own organization; public file listings closed.
  • Passwords of at least 8 letters and numbers, leaked-password checks, and session timeouts.
  • No secrets in source code; keys live in Vercel and Supabase settings. A redundant browser-exposed key name was removed.
  • Dependencies audited to zero known vulnerabilities, with automatic weekly update proposals.
  • Unused entry points removed — four unused offline-upload functions were deleted.
  • An incident-response runbook and a written data map are kept with the code.

Regulator registration

To be confirmed

Kenya. Certain data controllers and processors must register with the ODPC under the Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021. The duty can turn on factors beyond revenue — including processing children’s data at scale — so it should be checked with a Kenyan advocate or the ODPC portal before launch.

Nigeria. Nigeria’s regulator (the NDPC) expects “data controllers of major importance” to register and appoint a data protection officer, and larger controllers to file an annual compliance audit return. Whether the platform falls in that group has not yet been determined.

United States. COPPA has no registration; it requires notice, parental consent, and security and retention policies, which §04, §10, and §11 address.

Appendix & sources

Glossary

Data controller
Decides why and how personal data is processed; carries primary accountability under the Act.
Data processor
Processes data on a controller’s behalf and instructions (e.g. Supabase, Anthropic).
ODPC / NDPC
Kenya’s Office of the Data Protection Commissioner / Nigeria’s Data Protection Commission — the data protection regulators.
COPPA
The US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act, protecting children under 13.
ZDR
Zero Data Retention — an arrangement where API content is never stored at rest, offered by some vendors on request.
k-anonymization
Grouping records so each combination of traits covers at least k people, preventing re-identification of any one learner.
PII scrubbing
The §08 process that removes or converts personal information in a learner’s message before it reaches any AI model.
Scrub tier
Which of two rule sets (strict / community_helper) governs how much location detail survives scrubbing — see §08.
Safety flag
A classification (self-harm, harm to others, or hate/discriminatory content) raised by the §09 classifier, distinct from and in addition to the AI model’s own response.
