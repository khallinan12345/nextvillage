01

Scope & roles

This summary covers personal data collected through nextVillage’s learning platform — accounts, learning activity, AI chat/tutoring content, assessment results, and community-impact research data — for learners in partner communities such as Maji Mazuri. It maps that data against Kenya’s Data Protection Act, 2019 (“the Act”) and its General Regulations, 2021.

Under the Act, roles split roughly as follows:

PartyLikely roleWhat they hold
nextVillage (platform operator)Data controllerDecides what’s collected and why; accountable for the choices below.
Maji Mazuri & partner communitiesJoint controller (for their members)Issues the join code that enrolls their learners; best positioned to obtain guardian consent — see §10.
Supabase, Inc.Data processorPostgres database, authentication, file storage, edge functions.
Vercel Inc.Data processorApplication hosting, serverless API functions, cron jobs.
Anthropic, PBCData processor (sub-processor)Processes learner text sent to Claude for chat, tutoring, assessment, PII scrubbing, and safety classification.
Groq, Cerebras, Cloudflare, OpenRouter, Mistral, DeepSeekData processors (sub-processors)Free-tier AI fallback chain for a defined set of pages — see §07. Terms not yet vetted for this document.
ResendData processor (sub-processor)Transactional and authentication email, including the new safety-flag alerts in §09.

Nothing here is a legal opinion — treat the “likely role” column as a starting point for a Kenyan advocate or the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) to confirm, particularly the controller/joint-controller split with each partner community.

04

Retention

Gap No retention schedule exists today. Records — accounts, chat history, assessment results — are kept indefinitely unless someone manually deletes them.

The Act’s data-protection principles (commonly summarized from the Act’s Part IV) require that personal data be kept “no longer than necessary” for the purpose it was collected for. A defined schedule is what turns “we could delete this” into “we do delete this.” A reasonable starting point:

DataSuggested retentionThen
Active learner accountWhile the learner is active with a partner communityFlag for review after 12 months of inactivity
AI chat/tutoring transcripts90 days, or the life of the learning moduleDelete or aggregate into non-identifying progress metrics
Assessment resultsDuration of program participation + 1 grant cycleAnonymize for longitudinal research (k ≥ 5, already practiced)
Inactive/withdrawn accounts90 days after withdrawal or a deletion requestHard delete
Safety-flag logs (system_events)Not yet defined — see §09Should follow a separate, longer safeguarding schedule; needs its own decision, not the default above

These are starting figures for internal review, not settled policy — Maji Mazuri and any partner community should have a say in numbers that affect their members.

05

Deletion requests

Manual only There is no deletion request channel, form, or self-serve control yet. Today, an administrator removes a learner’s record directly in Supabase if asked — nothing is written down about how, or how fast.

What a documented process should specify:

  • How to ask. One clear channel (an email address or in-app request) rather than word-of-mouth to a facilitator.
  • Who can ask. The learner themselves, or a parent/guardian on behalf of a minor — see §10.
  • Turnaround. A stated target (30 days is a common, defensible benchmark; the Act does not fix a number, but ODPC expects a controller to act “without undue delay”).
  • What actually gets deleted. The Supabase row(s) via Row-Level-Security-scoped deletes, associated Storage objects, and — for any AI conversation content — nothing further to chase, since Anthropic’s own 30-day auto-purge already retires it (see §07).
  • What can’t be deleted immediately. Anonymized research data already folded into a k≥5 cohort, and a safety-flag record still open with a community leader, should be named as exceptions up front rather than discovered later.
06

Cross-border transfer

Unaddressed today Every message a Kenyan learner sends leaves Kenya — to Supabase and Anthropic in the US, through Vercel’s US-region functions, and for some pages, on to a second US-based provider. That’s ordinary for a SaaS product; the Act just requires a lawful basis for it.

Sections 48–49 of the Act permit a cross-border transfer on one of four bases: an adequacy finding for the destination country, appropriate safeguards (such as standard contractual clauses in the vendor’s data processing agreement), necessity for performing a contract with the data subject, or the data subject’s consent combined with confirmation that safeguards are in place. None of these has been formally documented for nextVillage yet — and now that §07 confirms more than one vendor is actually in the path, the safeguards question applies to each of them, not just Anthropic.

On moving hosting to Africa

Worth checking directly: Supabase does not currently offer an African hosting region. AWS itself has a Cape Town region (af-south-1), but Supabase briefly supported it during an early alpha and discontinued it — it isn’t offered to new or existing projects today. So “move Supabase to Africa” isn’t on the table as things stand.

Given that, the pragmatic path is not to chase regional hosting — it’s to make the existing US hosting lawful: execute data processing agreements with Supabase, Vercel, Anthropic, and (per §07) each free-tier fallback provider actually in the path, and add a plain-language consent notice at signup telling learners and guardians that their data is processed outside Kenya and under what protections. That’s a paperwork lift, not an infrastructure migration.

Reserve true data residency — self-hosting Postgres in af-south-1 or with a Kenyan provider — for if a funder or the ODPC later makes it a hard requirement; it’s a materially larger engineering project than this platform needs today.

07

AI providers & learner inputs

Anthropic’s own terms are solid — but the code audit this document called for is now done, and the answer needs action

Claude (Anthropic) — the confirmed processor for the default path

nextVillage’s learner-facing chat, tutoring, and assessment features call Claude models (claude-sonnet-5, claude-sonnet-4-6, claude-haiku-4-5) through the standard Claude API — Anthropic’s commercial terms, not a Zero-Data-Retention or “Covered Model” arrangement. Under those terms:

  • Prompts and outputs are automatically deleted within 30 days of receipt or generation.
  • Retained data is never used to train Anthropic’s models without nextVillage’s express permission.
  • The exception: content flagged by Anthropic’s automated trust-and-safety systems, or subject to a legal hold, may be retained up to 2 years.
  • Zero Data Retention (no storage at all, even briefly) is available on request from Anthropic’s sales team if nextVillage wants to remove even the 30-day window for learner conversations.

The rest of the story — a real fallback chain, confirmed in code

The previous revision of this document flagged an open question: does any learner-facing path fall through to one of the other providers configured in the codebase? Reading api/chat.js directly answers it: yes, for a defined set of pages. Requests from AILearningPage, EnglishSkillsPage, MathSkillsPage, ScienceSkillsPage, SkillsDevelopmentPage, AgricultureConsultantPage, FishingConsultantPage, EntrepreneurshipConsultantPage, AIAmbassadorsPage, and PidginTranslationModule route through a cost-driven fallback chain — Groq → Cerebras → Cloudflare Workers AI → OpenRouter (free tier) → Mistral → DeepSeek → Anthropic Haiku as the final, paid fallback — before a request ever reaches Anthropic. All coding-task pages, certification/evaluation pages, and (as of this revision) HealthcareNavigatorPage specifically route to Anthropic directly, no fallback chain involved.

Deliberate exception

HealthcareNavigatorPage was pulled out of the free-tier chain in this revision, specifically because health-adjacent conversation content is the most sensitive category this platform handles — it now always routes to Anthropic, never the fallback providers below. Confirmed directly in code (api/chat.js, FREE_TIER_PAGES exclusion with an explicit comment to that effect).

None of Groq’s, Cerebras’s, Cloudflare’s, OpenRouter’s, Mistral’s, or DeepSeek’s data-retention or training terms have been reviewed for this document — each is a separate sub-processor relationship the Act’s cross-border and safeguards requirements (§06) apply to independently. This is the single biggest change this revision makes to the AI picture: what was an open question is now a confirmed finding, and a new one (§13, item 02).

08

PII scrubbing New

Added this revision, in direct response to a request that no personal information beyond a learner’s own first name reach an AI model. Live on /api/chat, /api/chat-stream (the AI Playground’s streaming path), and the AI Image Creation function.

What’s allowed through, and what isn’t

The learner’s own first name is the only personal detail passed through unchanged. Everything else is either removed outright or converted to a relationship term before the message reaches any model:

Other people’s names
Converted to the relationship the text states or implies — “my brother,” “my neighbour,” “my patient” — never left in as a name, and never invented where the text gives none (“someone I know”).
Phone numbers, emails, handles
Removed by a deterministic pattern match, in any common format (not just US grouping — Kenyan and Nigerian formats are explicitly covered).
Addresses & locations
Governed by a scrub “tier” — see below.
School names, exact ages/birthdates
Removed in every case.

Two layers

A pattern match (regex) catches phone numbers, emails, and handles first — free, deterministic, and it runs regardless of whether the AI call that follows succeeds. A second pass sends the (already pattern-scrubbed) text to a Claude Haiku call that extracts a short list of “replace this exact phrase with that one” instructions, which this codebase applies itself — the model never rewrites a message in full, so its output stays small and bounded no matter how long the conversation has grown.

Location tiers

Blanket location scrubbing would break the pages where local specificity is the point — a health navigator that can’t know a learner is in Mathare can’t point at a clinic in Mathare. So the scrub runs in one of two modes depending on which page sent the request:

TierApplies toLocation handling
strict (default)AI Playground, Create Game, AI Image Creation, everywhere elseNothing more specific than a broad place name (a city, county, region) — a school or exact address is removed
community_helperAgriculture, Fishing, Healthcare Navigator, Entrepreneurship Consultant, AI Ambassadors — and their certification variantsNeighborhood/estate-level detail is kept; a full street address, school name, or P.O. Box is still removed

In both tiers, other people’s names are converted to a relationship regardless — the tier only ever changes how much location detail survives.

The precise guarantee — read this carefully

The extraction pass necessarily sends the (already pattern-scrubbed) text to Anthropic, to find what still needs removing — recognizing a name, or inferring “my brother” from context, is itself a language task a pattern match can’t do. So the accurate claim is “everyone except Anthropic sees only scrubbed text” — not “personal information never leaves the platform.” Concretely: raw (pattern-scrubbed, pre-name-extraction) learner text touches Anthropic briefly for the extraction call itself; the scrubbed result is what continues on to whichever model actually answers the learner — Anthropic directly, or one of §07’s fallback providers for the pages that route there. Anthropic’s own 30-day, no-training terms (§07) apply to that extraction call the same as any other Anthropic call.

Known gaps

  • Not yet cached across turns. Every request re-scrubs the entire conversation history, including messages already scrubbed on a prior turn — safe (re-scrubbing scrubbed text is a no-op) but not free. A cross-request cache keyed to a stable conversation ID would cut this further; not built yet.
  • Regex address matching is a floor, not a solution. It reliably catches P.O. Box / Plot / House-No. patterns and US-style street suffixes; an address with neither shape depends entirely on the second pass.
  • Coverage is scoped to the three functions named above. A handful of other Supabase Edge Functions that also call Anthropic directly for evaluation/scoring purposes (challenge submissions, weekly-challenge generation) were not touched in this revision and do not yet have this scrub layer in front of them — confirmed by a direct search of supabase/functions/: none of them import or reference the scrubbing logic. See §13, item 03.
  • The image-creation scrubber is a separate, mirrored copy, not a shared import. supabase/functions/generate-image/index.ts reimplements the same regex + extraction logic rather than calling api/_lib/piiScrubbing.js — the two can drift out of sync as either is updated, since a fix applied to one side doesn’t automatically reach the other.
09

Safety guardrails & escalation New

Added this revision, as a direct safeguarding response: a provision for a learner using the AI inappropriately toward themselves or someone else. This is a genuinely new capability, not a policy restated — it’s worth describing to Maji Mazuri directly as part of onboarding, since it changes what a partner community can expect the platform to do on their behalf.

How it works

Independently of the AI’s own reply, every message is classified by a separate Claude Haiku call into one of four categories: none, self_harm, harm_to_others, or hate_or_discriminatory. This classifier reads the learner’s original, unscrubbed message — deliberately: a flag reading “I’m going to hurt [name removed]” would be close to useless to the person meant to act on it.

  • In chat (AI Playground, Create Game): a flag doesn’t block or change the AI’s own response — the model’s own trained, caring reply still reaches the learner in the moment. The flag instead fires a background alert.
  • In AI Image Creation: a flagged prompt is refused outright — no image is generated — since an image request has no equivalent “helpful” fallback response the way a chat turn does.

Who gets told, and what they see

A flag emails the learner’s community leader(s) — resolved from the learner’s organization record, the same relationship the platform already uses to identify who runs a partner community — plus a fixed fallback address configured per environment, so a lookup failure never drops the alert silently. The email names the flagged category, the page, and a quoted excerpt of what was actually written; it also writes an internal log entry for an audit trail. It is explicitly labeled as unreviewed and automated — “please follow up with the student” — not a finding of fact.

What this changes for partner communities

This is the mechanism that answers a question Maji Mazuri or any partner should reasonably ask before onboarding: what happens if a young person says something alarming to the AI? The honest current answer is “a designated adult at your organization is emailed, automatically, usually within seconds” — worth stating plainly rather than leaving implicit, and worth confirming who that designated adult actually is for each partner before launch.

Known gaps

  • Confirmed still not done: a direct check of both environments’ secrets shows SAFETY_ALERT_FALLBACK_EMAIL is set on neither Vercel nor Supabase, and it is also absent from .env.local.example. In its current state, a flag for a learner whose organization has no leader profile on file goes to no one at all — the code’s own fallback logic returns early with nothing sent when both the leader list and the fallback list are empty.
  • Supabase’s copy of this alerting can’t send email at all today. RESEND_API_KEY exists in Vercel’s environment but is not present in the Supabase project’s secrets — so the mirrored safety-flag logic inside generate-image (§08) has no way to actually deliver an alert even once an organization leader is found.
  • Unverified against a live message. Every part of this has been reviewed and unit-tested against simulated responses; none of it has yet been exercised against a real Anthropic call with genuine flagged content. Worth a deliberate test pass before relying on it operationally.
  • No retention decision for the safety-event log itself — see the new row in §04.
10

Children’s data & consent

Not yet demonstrated The join-code signup ties a learner to an organization; it doesn’t, by itself, capture guardian consent.

Section 33 of the Act requires that personal data relating to a child (anyone under 18) be processed only with the consent of a parent or guardian, and in a manner that protects and advances the child’s best interests. Because nextVillage is built for young people in rural communities, this is likely the single highest-priority gap in this review.

The most workable fix given the existing model: fold guardian consent into the community’s own onboarding — Maji Mazuri (or any partner organization) collects it once as part of enrolling a group of learners and issuing join codes, rather than nextVillage trying to verify individual guardians at signup. That consent record, and what it covers, should be something nextVillage can point to on request.

§08 and §09 are relevant here, not a substitute: PII scrubbing and safety escalation are genuine, concrete protections worth naming in whatever a guardian is asked to consent to — but neither one is itself a consent mechanism, and neither closes this gap on its own.

11

Security controls already in place

Solid foundation

  • Encryption in transit and at rest — standard on Supabase-managed Postgres and Vercel-hosted traffic (TLS).
  • Row-Level Security (RLS) on Supabase tables — access is scoped per user/organization at the database layer, not left to application code alone.
  • No secrets committed to source — API keys and service-role credentials live in Vercel/Supabase environment variables; .env.local is git-ignored.
  • Join-code scoping — learners are bound to their organization at signup, limiting cross-community data exposure by construction.
  • PII scrubbing before any model call — new this revision; see §08.
  • Independent safety classification and leader escalation — new this revision; see §09.
  • Health-adjacent conversation routed to a single, contractually-reviewed provider only — HealthcareNavigatorPage no longer participates in the free-tier fallback chain; see §07.
12

ODPC registration

Unconfirmed

Kenya requires certain data controllers and processors to register with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner under the Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021. Registration duty can turn on factors beyond simple revenue thresholds — including processing children’s data at scale — so this is worth a direct check with a Kenyan advocate or the ODPC registration portal before launch, rather than assuming either way.

Appendix & sources

Glossary

Data controller
Decides why and how personal data is processed; carries primary accountability under the Act.
Data processor
Processes data on a controller’s behalf and instructions (e.g. Supabase, Anthropic).
ODPC
Office of the Data Protection Commissioner — Kenya’s data protection regulator.
ZDR
Zero Data Retention — an Anthropic arrangement where API content is never stored at rest, available on request.
k-anonymization
Grouping records so each combination of traits covers at least k people, preventing re-identification of any one learner.
PII scrubbing
The §08 process that removes or converts personal information in a learner’s message before it reaches any AI model.
Scrub tier
Which of two rule sets (strict / community_helper) governs how much location detail survives scrubbing — see §08.
Safety flag
A classification (self-harm, harm to others, or hate/discriminatory content) raised by the §09 classifier, distinct from and in addition to the AI model’s own response.
