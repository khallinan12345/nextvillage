# Leader training and sign-off

**For:** every group leader and mentor, before they supervise learners. Takes about three hours, in two
sittings, with the child-protection lead or an experienced leader.

**How to use it:** read each module aloud together, discuss the questions, then complete the check and the
sign-off at the end. Keep the signed page. Repeat each year.

The longer reference is the **Leader Guide**. This training is the short version that every leader must
know before the first session.

## Module 1: Why we are here (15 minutes)

nextVillage gives young people a route to learning beyond what school offers, using AI as a patient
tutor. The leader makes it work: you open the space, welcome children, keep them safe, and help them when
the AI is not enough. The AI never replaces you.

**Discuss:** What would you want a leader to do for your own child?

## Module 2: Keeping children safe (45 minutes)

Read the child safeguarding policy together, then cover:

- The four steps: make the child safe, listen and record, report the same day, follow up.
- What a concern looks like (policy section 3).
- Never promise secrecy. Never investigate alone.
- How to receive a safety alert email and what to do the same day.
- The code of conduct: never alone with a child, no private messages, no gifts, no photos for personal use.

**Practice (role-play, in pairs):**

1. A 12-year-old says another adult at home hits her. What do you say? What do you write?
2. You receive a safety alert saying a learner wrote about wanting to hurt himself. What do you do today?
3. A visiting volunteer offers a boy a lift home. What do you do?

**Check:** Can each leader say who the child-protection lead is, and her phone number, without looking?

## Module 3: Consent and children's information (30 minutes)

- Every learner under 18 needs a signed guardian consent form. Where a parent has no email, the form is
  signed in person or by thumbprint with a witness.
- Hand every family the **My path card** with the consent form, so parents know what the programme is for.
- Record the consent on the leader dashboard. The dashboard shows who is still missing a form.
- Young learners under 13 are better protected by default: no peer chat, names hidden, and a reviewed AI
  provider only. Ask the birth year of each child so the right protections apply.
- Never share a child's name, photo, work or story outside the programme without written permission.
- Parents can see, correct or delete their child's records, or withdraw consent, by telling you in person
  or contacting the child-protection lead.

**Check:** Where do you record consent? What do you do if a parent asks to withdraw?

## Module 4: Running a session (45 minutes)

- Open and close the space. Take a register.
- Welcome new learners. Show them how to log in with their own account (one person, one account).
- If a learner forgot their password, use "Email me a link to reset my password". Never let them make a
  second account.
- Walk around. Watch for learners who are stuck, distracted, or being unkind to each other.
- Help learners think; do not give the answer. Ask what they have tried.
- Keep personal details out of the AI. Learners should not type phone numbers, addresses, or other
  people's private details.
- End by asking each learner what they learned.

**Check:** What do you say to a learner who has lost their password?

## Module 5: Learner results (20 minutes)

Read "How students are evaluated". Remember:

- Scores are an estimate from an AI, not a grade.
- Spelling, grammar, accent and Pidgin are not marked.
- Use scores to start a kind conversation, not to compare or shame children.
- A low score with high effort means the child needs support, not blame.

**Check:** A child scored low. What do you do first?

## Module 6: Working with the platform team (15 minutes)

- Tell the platform team about technical problems, wrong scores, or learners who cannot sign in.
- The platform team will never ask for a learner's password. Neither should you.
- Do not share your own login. Sign out on shared computers.
- If you lose a phone or laptop with your login, tell the team the same day.
- Report anything that worries you about the platform, even if you are not sure.

## Module 7: When something goes wrong (20 minutes)

- A learner is in danger: safeguarding policy, section 5.
- A data problem (wrong person saw a child's information, lost device): tell the platform team and the
  child-protection lead at once. The incident runbook explains what happens next.
- A learner is upset or angry: stay calm, keep the group safe, talk privately in the open, and tell the
  lead if it was serious.
- A parent complains: listen, thank them, say you will pass it on, and tell the lead.

## Leader check (10 questions)

Answer in your own words. The lead checks the answers with you; pass mark is 9 of 10, and any missed
question is talked through.

1. Who is the child-protection lead and how do you contact her?
2. A child tells you something frightening. List what you do, in order.
3. Why must you never promise a child to keep a secret?
4. Name three things the code of conduct forbids.
5. A safety alert email arrives. What do you do the same day?
6. Where do you record a guardian's consent?
7. Why are learners under 13 treated differently?
8. A child lost their password. What do you do?
9. What can you do if a child scores low?
10. Who do you tell if you lose the phone you use for the platform?

## For the trainer

- Keep it a conversation. Ask about local examples.
- The role-plays are the most important part; allow time for them.
- Where a leader finds a module hard to read, read it to them and talk it through. Reading ability must
  never stop a good leader.
- Tell the platform team if a module does not fit local practice, so it can be adapted (change process).
