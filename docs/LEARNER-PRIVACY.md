# Your information, in simple words

**For learners.** This is the same text that is at the top of the privacy page on the website
(www.nextvillage.community/privacy). Leaders: read it aloud with new learners, in English or in the
language they know best, and use the questions at the end. A learner who cannot read well should still
understand it.

Version 1, 17 October 2026.

## What to know

1. **This is your space to learn.** We keep a little information about you so the site works: your name,
   your town, the year you were born, and what you write and make.
2. **Your leader and the nextVillage team can see your work** so they can help you. Other learners cannot
   see your private work.
3. **When you talk to the AI, it does not need your phone number, your address, or your family's
   secrets. Do not type them.** If you do by mistake, tell your leader.
4. **The AI can be wrong.** Check important things with a person you trust.
5. **Never share your password.** If you forget it, press "Email me a link to reset my password." Do not
   make a second account.
6. **If something online makes you feel scared, sad or uncomfortable, tell your leader or an adult you
   trust, right away.** You will not be in trouble.
7. **If you write that you are in danger, or want to hurt yourself or someone else, a leader will be told**
   so that they can help you.
8. **We never sell your information and we never show you advertising.**
9. **Your parent or guardian can ask to see your information, change it, or delete it.** You can ask your
   leader too.
10. **Questions?** Ask your leader, or talk to Confidence Onuoha.

## For leaders: how to teach this (10 minutes)

1. Read the ten points slowly. Pause after each and ask, "What does that mean for you?"
2. Ask: "What is one thing you should never type to the AI?" (phone number, address, family secrets,
   passwords).
3. Ask: "Who do you tell if something online scares you?" (a leader or an adult they trust).
4. Ask: "What do you do if you forget your password?" (reset link, not a new account).
5. Show a learner how to find the reset button on the login page.
6. Tell the learners clearly: **telling an adult about something scary is brave and never gets a child in
   trouble.**

For learners under 13, repeat this at the start of the first three sessions, using pictures or short role
plays rather than reading.

## Translations

If you translate this into a local language or Pidgin, ask someone who speaks it well to check the
meaning, especially points 3, 6 and 7. Send corrections to the platform team so the shared version
improves.
